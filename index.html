The bit nobody tells you

Most yiros is assembled. Ours is made.

You've had the 1am servo version. The food-court version. The one where the meat arrives frozen, pre-formed and pressed into a cone by a machine in a factory, then spun under an electric element until it's grey at the edges. It's fine. It's fuel. It's also the reason you've never thought of yiros as something you'd sit down for.

Why this bloke, and why here

Mr Spiros isn't real. Everything he stands for is.

Mr Spiros is a character — a stand-in for the way Peter Gloftis grew up eating. Macedonian-Greek household, Gold Coast. Easter, Christmas, name days, long Sunday lunches, and always a whole lamb turning by hand over coals for hours while everyone waited.

He's done this before

Peter is the original creator of The Lamb Shop, in Broadbeach and Brisbane. He's no longer involved with that brand — this one is the personal version.

It's in the family

His cousin Simon Gloftis is behind Hellenika and The Fish House. Greek food on this coast runs in the bloodline.

Small on purpose

One venue. No branches. No franchise plan. Peter chose Nobby Beach for its sleepy beachside charm and wants to keep it that size — quality and conversation over scale.

Walls the colour of the Aegean

It's a small room at the back of a beautiful building, painted the blue of the sea Peter's family came from. It seats a fraction of what the front of the precinct does. That's deliberate.
